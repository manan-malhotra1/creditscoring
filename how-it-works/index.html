Ecocash Credit Scoring Engine
One page
How it works

From a daily file drop
to a credit decision

Every night, Ecocash files land in Azure. By morning each customer carries a score and a set of features. When an application arrives, a rule engine turns that score into a yes with an amount, or a no. This page shows the whole path, and exactly which numbers the credit team controls.

Layer A
The scoring model

Ranks how likely a customer is to repay. Runs overnight in Microsoft Fabric, produces one number between 0 and 100. It never makes a decision and never sees a credit policy.

Owned by data science. Changes with a retrain, not a setting.
Layer B
The rule engine

Turns that number into an offer: approve or decline, and where it approves, a limit, a tenure and a deposit. All credit policy lives here. No referral step in phase 1.

Owned by the credit team. 65 rules, each editable without a code change.

1The data that arrives

Ecocash drops files into Azure blob storage each day. Nothing is computed live at application time: everything the engine reads was prepared in the last overnight cycle.

2Layer A: the score

Two scores are produced from the same features. A fixed points model, which works for anyone, and a trained model, which is sharper but needs enough history. Both land on the same 0 to 100 scale.

The trained model

A CatBoost regressor, retrained offline and stored as an artefact. It scores in batch overnight, so at application time the answer is simply looked up.

3Three paths, and nobody is turned away for being new

L0 decides which of three routes a customer takes. It routes, it never declines. A customer the model cannot rank is scored another way, and a customer with nothing at all still gets a first offer.

Path 1
Scored

Every sufficiency threshold met. The trained model ranks them and the band table sets the offer, up to the full product maximum.

Score from the model Bands A to E Up to $500
The normal path. Everything in Layer B applies in full.
Path 2
Thin file: the fallback scorecard

Some history, but not enough for the model to rank them. Eight observed signals are scored on points and the total enters the same 0 to 100 band table. Deterministic arithmetic, no training.

Capped at band C, because a points total is a rougher estimate than a model score. Starter limit $50. The scorecard is a bridge, not a permanent parallel system: it is retired for a segment once the model can score it.
Path 3
No file: starter limit and the ladder

Nothing to score at all. They are not rejected. A small starter limit is offered and every loan repaid in full raises it by half again, until normal band scoring takes over.

Starts at $30 over 3 months with a 30% deposit, rises 50% per repaid cycle, ceiling $250. Falling 30 days past due resets to the start. Set the starter limit to zero and this group declines instead, by policy arithmetic rather than a gate.

4Layer B: the decision waterfall

Eight layers, evaluated top to bottom, cheapest and most decisive first. Two rules govern the whole engine: any layer can stop the process, and limits only ever go down.

5What actually sets the amount

The final limit is not calculated, it is selected. Every layer contributes a ceiling and the lowest one wins. This is why a weaker customer is approved with a small limit rather than declined.

final limit = MINIMUM ( band limit, affordability limit, product maximum, customer exposure cap, tier cap )

6What you can change, and what you cannot

The clean split between the two layers is the point of the design. One side moves with a retrain; the other moves with a setting, the same afternoon.
